# Tells

Machine cadence. Hunt these on every lining pass. The checker catches the kill-list. You catch the rest.

## Kill-list (checker)

These strings fail `prose-check.mjs` when they appear in narration. Dialogue may still trip them; rewrite the line.

- delve, tapestry, underscore (verb), pivotal, embark, nestled, vibrant, realm
- testament to, a dance of, could not help but, couldn't help but
- little did, and then everything changed
- the air was thick, words hung in the air
- a smile played, something inside him, something inside her
- profound, moreover, furthermore, utilize, necessitate
- landscape of, in the tapestry, the very fabric

Unicode em dash (`—`) and en dash (`–`). Zero allowed.

## Patterns the checker cannot see

- **Gerund stacking**: three `-ing` clauses in one sentence. Break it.
- **Lazy triad**: brave, loyal, and kind. Unless the third item bites, cut to one.
- **Staccato drama**: four short sentences in a row for effect. Mix the lengths.
- **Elegant variation**: cycling "the marshal," "the older man," "the Kessari officer" because you fear repeating a name. Repeat the name.
- **Aphorism**: "Silence is the language of fear." Characters do not speak fortune cookies unless that is their flaw.
- **Significance inflation**: "what happened next would change everything." Let the event be the event.
- **Show then tell**: fist on the table, then "she was angry." Keep the fist.
- **Hedging**: somewhat, a sense of, seemed to, appeared to. Commit, or let the POV be unsure on purpose.
- **Filter stack**: she saw, she heard, she realized, she felt. The camera is already in their head.
- **Summary breath ending**: the last sentence restates the chapter's meaning. End on a cost, a door, a lie, a body.
- **Even tone**: every paragraph equally polished. Let one be ugly. Let one be tired.
- **MFA dialogue**: complete sentences, no overlap, exposition dressed as talk. Cut to what they would actually say while working.
- **Safe metaphor**: moonlight like silver, silence like a blanket, time standing still. Steal the metaphor from this POV's job.
- **Inventory description**: hair, eyes, clothes, then personality. One telling detail, then motion.

## Positive replacements

- Em dash breaks become a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses.
- "She felt dread" becomes what her hands do to the console.
- "The political landscape shifted" becomes who lost a vote, who left the room.
- "A testament to their bond" becomes the ugly thing they still do for each other.

## False positives

Do not flatten voice to dodge the list. A character who says "moreover" ironically keeps it. A quoted document may be stiff on purpose. Mark those lines as voice, not lining failures. The checker still has to pass; rephrase rather than argue with the oracle.
