# Scene

A scene is a unit of change. Enter late. Leave early.

## Entry

Start on pressure. Not weather. Not a wake-up. The POV is already in the middle of a job, a lie, or a wait that has gone on too long.

First-look at a place uses the senses the POV's job trained. A fusion tech smells ozone and hears the wrong harmonic. A marshal counts exits. A scientist watches who is allowed to touch the instrument.

Familiar-look skips the tour. Name what changed since last time.

## During

- **Want**: the POV is trying to get a concrete thing in this scene.
- **Cost**: another person with a legitimate interest can take something.
- **Turn**: knowledge, leverage, or the body is different at the end.

Dialogue does two jobs: the spoken job and the real one. People talk while working. They do not take turns delivering essays.

Interiority is short and biased. Free indirect: the narration uses the POV's words for the world. Mira does not think "the Resonance Architecture." She thinks about the coherence numbers that will not sit.

White space is a tool. A one-line paragraph is a flinch.

## Exit

Leave on the turn. Do not explain what it meant. Do not preview the next chapter's theme.

If the scene only delivered world facts, it failed. Put the fact in the way of the want.

## Pacing

Action: short sentences, concrete verbs, fewer names.
Talk: overlapping, unfinished, specific nouns.
Dread: the procedure continues. The checklist is the horror.

Do not write a fight as a video game. Bodies miss. Gear fails. People freeze.

## Chapter

Target 2,500 to 4,500 words unless the brief says otherwise. One dominant scene, maybe a coda. Two locations is plenty.

Title is optional. File name is `ch-NN.md` with zero-padded number.
