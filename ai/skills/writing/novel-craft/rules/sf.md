# Science fiction page

This book is lived-in hard-adjacent SF. Wormholes, polities, laces, the Threshold. The vault already decided how the universe works. The page shows people using it.

## Technology

Do not describe a machine. Describe the work around it.

If you introduce a capability, spend the next beat on who loses power because of it. A gate that opens cheaply kills a hauler guild. A lace that binds a fire team also binds their nightmares. Follow the ripple one step. Stop. The Systems Architect move is cause and effect, not a spec sheet.

Limits are fuel. Wormholes take stations, time, and a collapse window. Write the queue, the fear of the late transit, the person who did not make it.

No infodump. A new reader may be lost for a page. That is allowed. Orient through the POV's job, one object at a time.

## Scale

Do not tour the galaxy. One room can hold a civilization if the mail is late and the gate fees went up.

Names of polities appear when a person would use them. Not as a roll call.

## Aliens

When non-humans arrive, do not reach for wings-and-halos as decoration if the vault already gave you that image. Commit to what it does to human nervous systems, protocol, and weapons policy. Angelic is a human word. The POV will use a worse one.

Avoid thesaurus aliens. Avoid explaining their philosophy in a courtyard speech. They want something. They have a body that makes some things easy and some things expensive.

## Science

Stay inside vault physics. If you need a new mechanism, stop and ticket **world-vault**. Do not improvise a particle.

Jargon is fine when the POV would think it. Gloss once, in action, not in a parenthetical lecture.

## Tone

Political. Bodily. Tired. People have been living with this tech for centuries. Wonder is for the Threshold when it finally does something. Everything else is work, rank, and money.
